## 4. Risk disclosures

Trading is risky. These are some of the risks, not all of them.

### 4.1 Options

- Before trading options, read the Options Clearing Corporation's **Characteristics and Risks of Standardized Options**: <https://www.theocc.com/company-information/documents-and-archives/options-disclosure-document>. Your broker must give it to you before approving options trading.
- A long option can **expire worthless**; you can lose 100% of the premium paid, often within days.
- Out-of-the-money and illiquid contracts may have **no bid** at all. You may be unable to sell at any price before expiration.
- Wide bid-ask spreads cost you on the way in and on the way out. Implied volatility can collapse after an event ("IV crush") and lower an option's price even when the stock moves your way. Time decay speeds up near expiration.
- Short option legs can be **assigned early**, and some strategies carry losses that are large or unlimited.

### 4.2 Stocks and ETFs

Prices can fall sharply or to zero. Trading can be halted. Gaps at the open can skip past your stop. Leveraged and inverse ETFs can lose value quickly and are not meant to be held long term.

### 4.3 Futures and leverage

Futures are leveraged. A small price move can cause a large loss, **losses can exceed your deposit**, and your broker can liquidate your positions without notice to meet margin.

### 4.4 Foreign exchange (forex)

Retail forex is leveraged (US rules cap leverage at 50:1 on major pairs and 20:1 on others). **Losses can exceed your deposit.** Retail forex is not traded on a regulated exchange, and only CFTC-registered dealers may offer it to US residents.

### 4.5 Crypto assets and on-chain tokens

Crypto assets are extremely volatile and largely unregulated. They are **not** protected by SIPC or FDIC. On-chain tokens can be abandoned, drained, or "rug pulled"; smart contracts can fail or be exploited; transactions cannot be reversed; and pools can be too thin to exit. You can lose everything.

### 4.6 Prediction markets and event contracts

Event contracts can settle at zero. Trade them only on venues that are lawful where you live, such as CFTC-regulated exchanges where they are permitted. Some states restrict or prohibit them.

### 4.7 Day trading

Frequent trading increases costs and risk. Under FINRA's pattern day trader rule, a margin account that makes four or more day trades in five business days must keep at least **$25,000** in equity, or it can be restricted.

# Earnings put-buying backtest — MRNA & CRWD (last 8 quarters each)

**APPROXIMATION — read the limits before the numbers.** Option prices are modeled with Black-Scholes, not real historical quotes. IV at entry = 1.3 x 30-day realized volatility (an earnings-premium proxy); positions are repriced at exit with IV = 1.0 x realized vol to mimic the post-earnings IV crush. Strike = entry-day close (exact ATM); expiry = the Friday of the exit week. Risk-free rate 4%. No bid/ask spread, no commissions, no slippage — real fills would be worse. $1,000 of premium per event, fractional contracts allowed. Data: Yahoo Finance daily closes (Stooq unreachable from this sandbox; desk Alpaca adapter has no keys configured).

## What the tape actually did

## Verdict

Buying ATM puts the day before earnings on MRNA and CRWD (strategy B, with a realistic 1.3x earnings IV premium and post-earnings IV crush) would have **made $6,452** on $16,000 staked over the last 8 quarters — a win rate of 8/16. Buying 5 days early (A) did not help: $1,813 total. Even with NO IV premium at all (B-sens, IV = realized vol), the puts total $13,241 — and even then the hit rate was barely better than a coin flip, with the profit concentrated in a few big down moves. The straddle benchmark (C), which pays on a big move either way, totals $6,808.

Bottom line: on these two names over the last two years, 'buy puts before earnings' was not a money machine. The occasional crash paid, but most quarters the stock went up, went sideways, or fell less than the premium + IV crush required — and this model is generous (no spreads or fees).
